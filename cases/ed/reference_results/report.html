ED Flow Simulation

A computer model of how patients move through an emergency department. It was built from an event log, checked against that log, and then used to test what happens when staffing, beds or demand change.

The data are synthetic. The event log came from a generator with assumed parameters, because no real hospital log was available. The numbers below show that the method works. They say nothing about any real hospital.

How the model works

The model runs a simulated ED for 120 days. Follow one patient left to right. Each step has something that limits it, and the patient waits in line when that thing is busy.

Every duration is a random draw from a curve fitted to the log, so repeated runs give a realistic spread of outcomes.

Where each number comes from
  • Measured from the log: arrival rate by hour and weekday, acuity mix, how long each step takes, share who get tests, share admitted, and how long patients wait before leaving.
  • Assumed by me, because a log cannot show staff or beds: physicians, triage nurses, ED beds, ward beds and imaging machines.
  • Tuned until the model matched: time physicians spend on reassessment and average inpatient stay (4.2 days). The log does not record either.

What the model says

The bottleneck is inpatient beds, not ED beds

206→16 min

Cutting inpatient stay by 10% drops average boarding from 206 to 16 minutes and unseen departures from 4.5% to 2.5%. Adding 4 ED beds gives no measurable gain. Inpatient beds run at 94% busy, ED beds at 70%.

More physicians fix waiting, and expose boarding

131→44 min

One extra physician per shift cuts the 90th percentile wait for a physician from 131 to 44 minutes and physician load from 91% to 70%. Boarding rises from 206 to 354 minutes, because patients reach the admit decision sooner and the ward is still full.

10% more patients overwhelms the current setup

4.5→18.8%

Patients who leave unseen climb from 4.5% to 18.8%. The 90th percentile wait for a physician reaches 236 minutes and average boarding 679 minutes.

A combined package absorbs the extra demand

3.0% unseen

One more physician per shift, 10% shorter inpatient stay and 25% faster lab and imaging bring the 90th percentile wait to 56 minutes at +10% demand, with about 168 patients served per day (baseline 152). Inpatient beds are still 94% busy, so there is little slack left.

Does the model match the real log?

It passes 24 of 28 checks. The model and the log agree on patient volume, length of stay, boarding, share admitted and share who leave unseen. The model makes patients wait longer for a physician than the log does, mostly low-acuity ones.

Real logModel (6 runs of 120 days)

Each curve shows the share of patients finished by a given time. Where the two lines sit close together, the model reproduces the real log. Length of stay and boarding overlap closely. Door to physician drifts apart in the middle of the range.

The four misses
  • Overall door to physician: model mean 57 min against 42 in the log (+36%), median +30%, 90th percentile +35%.
  • Acuity level 4 median: 38 min in the model against 23 in the log. Levels 2 and 3 match within 10 to 20%.
  • Cause not yet identified. The first place to look is how physician time is shared among low-acuity patients.

Progress toward the 80% utilization target

The goal is to run resources near 80% busy so there is room for surges. ED beds are already below it. Physicians sit near 91% at baseline and fall to 70 to 78% with an extra physician. Inpatient beds stay above 80% in every scenario, and only shorter inpatient stays bring them close (85%). Reaching 80% there would take more inpatient beds or faster discharge.

What to trust, and what to check next

  • Staffing and bed counts are inputs. An event log cannot reveal how many physicians or beds there were. The model uses 4, 4 and 2 physicians across three shifts, 30 ED beds and 93 inpatient beds. The 91% physician load follows from those assumptions and is not validated.
  • Two quantities were calibrated, not observed. Time spent on reassessment and nursing care (scale 0.28) and average inpatient stay (4.2 days) were tuned until the model matched the log. Scenarios that change them carry more uncertainty.
  • Some resources are not limited. ED nurses, lab stations and porters have no capacity in the model, and lab and imaging reports are pure delays.
  • One site, one ED area. There is no fast-track zone, resuscitation bay, shift-change effect or time-of-day ward discharge.
  • Next steps. Swap in a real log, close the door-to-physician gap, then rebuild the same model in AnyLogic and compare the two against each other and against the log.
